\chapter{Patient Study}\minitoc\newpage\label{chap.5}
With the presented chapters on theory of fluid dynamics, the brain arterial system and the description of a new virtual stent deployment method, one complex patient study is going to be analyzed. The case has already been used in the stent deployment to assess the performance of the method. Here we will further develop on the hemodynamic effects in it and explore the implications of the FDS treatment, which, sadly, can be related to the cause of death of the patient. To support our and the clinicians understanding, we will evaluate pre- and poststented configurations in regards to common remodelling indicators and lastly consider a novel simulation including wall compliance. The latter simulation has been done in cooperation with Aurèle Goetz, whose work is dedicated to the Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) modelling of intracranial aneurysms.

\section{Patient medical record}\label{chap5:sec1}
The patient is a woman born in January 1963, with no history of smoking, hypertension, nor family history of intracranial aneurysms. Vertigo along with anxiety and a strange sensation lead her to seek consultation of a neurologist. After a first consultation, she was appointed to do an MRI examination of the crane, revealing a giant intracranial aneurysm compressing of the left temporal lobe\footnote{Temporal lobes: Regions of the brain situated on both halves above the ears.}. Her neurologist referred her to a neuro-interventionalist in the same month for further evaluation. A cerebral arteriography confirmed the existance of a giant aneurysm in the supra-clinoid segment of the left ICA. The englobing of the parent artery by the aneurysm cause a delayed opacification of the left middle cerebral artery. The small anterior communicating artery was visible during compression of the left ICA. 

Based on the presence of symptoms and a menace of rupture, endovascular treatment was proposed to the patient, to which she agreed during the following medical consultation. One week before the procedure, she began taking aspirin (75 mg/day) and prasugrel\footnote{Prasugrel: Platelet aggregation inhibitor medication.} (10 mg/day) to reduce the risk of blood clots. The procedure started with a balloon test occlusion, where a balloon was temporarily inflated in the artery to block blood flow. A guiding catheter was placed in both the right and left internal carotid arteries. A Scepter XC 4--11 balloon was positioned in the left ICA upstream of the aneurysm and inflated to stop blood flow. Contrast injection via the right catheter showed a delay of over one second in the opacification of the left cerebral veins compared to the right, indicating that the left carotid artery could not be safely occluded. Therefore, preserving and reconstructing the left carotid artery with a flow-diverting stent was necessary. A $3\times21$\,mm FRED Jr flow-diverter was deployed from the left carotid terminus to the left internal carotid artery upstream of the aneurysm, immediately causing contrast stasis in the aneurysm sac. Upon waking up, the patient showed no neurological deficits and continued antiplatelet therapy. At her follow-up 1.5 months later, her clinical exam returned normal results. Six months after the procedure, a follow-up cerebral arteriography showed a patent\footnote{Patent (stent): Maintaining the openness and integrity of the vessel or structure where it has been placed.} stent with slight narrowing at its origin and unchanged opacification of the aneurysm sac. Continued dual antiplatelet therapy was advised, with a repeat arteriography scheduled for ten months post-procedure.

Eighth months after the intervention, the patient reported a sudden headache. A brain CT scan revealed spontaneous hyperdensity in the aneurysm, suggesting thrombosis (blood clot). A week later, she suffered a sudden coma, with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 4, indicating severe brain injury. A CT scan revealed subarachnoid hemorrhage (bleeding in the space surrounding the brain), intraventricular hemorrhage, and a left temporal hematoma\footnote{Hematoma: A solid swelling of clotted blood within the tissues.}. Cerebral arteriography showed persistent aneurysm opacification, a patent stent, and active contrast leakage from the aneurysm sac's postero-inferior part. A microcatheter was placed in the aneurysm sac through the stent mesh, and the sac was occluded with coils, halting the bleeding and completely occluding the aneurysm. Sadly, the patient passed away five days after this second intervention.
