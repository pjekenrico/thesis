\subsection{Inlet Boundary Conditions}\label{chap3:sec2:subsec:1}
The brain is supplied with blood through 3 main arteries: the left and right internal carotid arteries (ICA) and the basilar artery (BA). The ICAs are the main supplier of blood to the brain, while the BA is the result of the fusion of the left and right vertebral arteries (VA). The ICA and BA are connected through the Circle of Willis (CoW), a network of arteries that ensures the blood supply to the brain even in case of occlusion of one of the main arteries. The CoW, as discussed in the first section, harbours most of IA or is located close to them. In most simulations of this disease there is one supplier vessel involved, which branches into several vessels that become outlets. Nevertheless, since the CoW is a ring and thus contains redundancies, it is possible to have more suppliers. The latter case is included in the third chapter of this work.

Blood arriving from the proximal vessels is of strongly pulsatile nature due to the inherent functioning of the driving pump, the heart.
