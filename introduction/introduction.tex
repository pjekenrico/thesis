\section{First Section} \label{intro:sec1}

Intracranial Aneurysms (IAs) are abnormal bulging or ballooning in the walls of blood vessels within the brain. These vascular anomalies can develop in any part of the brain's arterial network, most commonly found at arterial bifurcations where vessels branch out. Although some aneurysms remain asymptomatic and may go unnoticed, they hold the potential to cause serious health complications.

The primary concern associated with IAs is the risk of rupture. When an aneurysm ruptures, it leads to a condition known as SubArachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH), which involves bleeding into the subarachnoid space — the area between the brain and the thin tissues that cover it. The sudden increase in pressure from the bleeding can compress and damage nearby brain tissue, leading in 50\% of the cases to the patient's death \cite{Keedy2006}. In half of the remaining cases permanent disabilities are observed after surviving a SAH.
